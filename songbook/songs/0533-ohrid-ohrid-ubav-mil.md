# Охрид, Охрид убав мил

*Ohrid, Ohrid ubav mil* · *Ohrid, Ohrid, dear and beautiful*

Pesna.org #533: <https://pesna.org/song/533>

**Notes.** Вистер бил виенски виолинист кој заедно со семејството, кое се состоело од музичари, настапувале во Охридските ресторани и хотели меѓу двете светски војни. Песната ја компонирал Сотир Ѕингал, опишувајќи ја случката кога заедно со уште двајца охриѓани („Борис од Мицета от Карајлија и Илче от попа Анастаса“ - линк) го украле изнајменото кајче од Вистер и го преминале езерото до Албанија.

## Background

The song was composed by Sotir Dzingal. It describes an episode in which he and two other men from Ohrid took a boat they had rented from Vister and crossed the lake to Albania. Vister was a Viennese violinist who, with his family of musicians, played in the restaurants and hotels of Ohrid between the two world wars. Mood: nostalgic, a farewell to the home town and the mother.

## English

*A composed song with a known author, so there is no line-by-line English here: a summary and a word list instead, so you can follow along.*

**Your translation.** Written by you, not by me. I checked it line by line against the Cyrillic.

> Verse 1:
> Ohrid, Ohrid, dear and beautiful,
> you have been a paradise to me.
> I am going far away,
> I know not if I shall return again.
>
> Verse 2:
> My beautiful lake,
> I left it in tears.
> In Vister's little boat,
> I found myself in Albania.
>
> Verse 3:
> Farewell, mother, farewell forever,
> I am going far away.
> I am going far away,
> I know not if I shall return again.
>
> Verse 4 (closing salute):
> Ohrid, Ohrid, dear and beautiful,
> city of Kliment, of Samuil.

**What it is about.** Composer: Sotir Dzingal (Ѕингал), per the input notes, describing the episode in which he and two other Ohrid men took a boat rented from the Viennese violinist Vister and crossed the lake to Albania. Mood: nostalgic, a farewell to the home town and mother. Verse 1: the singer tells Ohrid it was paradise for him and that he is leaving far away, not knowing if he will come back. Verse 2: he left the beautiful lake in tears and ended up in Albania by means of Vister's boat. Verse 3: he says goodbye to his mother and repeats that he is going far and may not return. Verse 4: a closing salute to Ohrid as the city of Kliment and Samuil. Grammar and dialect: ѕ in Ѕингал and the clipped дал' are colloquial; the genitive-looking 'од Вистера' means 'belonging to Vister'; 'веч' in 'збогум веч' is my reading as 'anymore/already', not certain; the repeated ќе + present (ќе одам) forms the future.
