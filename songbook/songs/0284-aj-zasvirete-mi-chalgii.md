# Ај засвирете ми чалгии

*Aj zasvirete mi čalgii* · *Come On, Play Me Chalgia Tunes*

Pesna.org #284: <https://pesna.org/song/284>

## Background

Credited to Jonče Hristovski in your songbook. *Čalgija* is the old urban ensemble of violin, oud, kanun, clarinet and hand drum heard in the cafés of Skopje, Bitola and Ohrid.

## English

*A composed song with a known author, so there is no line-by-line English here: a summary and a word list instead, so you can follow along.*

**Your translation.** Written by you, not by me. I checked it line by line against the Cyrillic.

> Verse 1:
> Come, play for me, chalgia players,
> a song full of merak,
> it is a feast day for my soul,
> today I weep for my youth.
>
> Verse 2 (refrain):
> Play for me, chalgia players,
> play me everything from the heart,
> it is a feast day for my soul,
> today I grieve for my youth.
>
> Verse 3:
> Come, play for me, chalgia players,
> a heavy song like life itself,
> so that I fill my eyes with tears,
> so that my heart is eased.
>
> Verse 4 (refrain):
> Play for me, chalgia players,
> play me everything from the heart,
> it is a feast day for my soul,
> today I mourn for my youth.

**What it is about.** Composer and lyricist: Jonče Hristovski (1931-2000), per your 6-song songbook. A chalgija is the old urban ensemble of violin, oud, kanun, clarinet and hand drum from the cafés of Skopje, Bitola and Ohrid. Mood: wistful, tender, nostalgic. Verse 1: the singer asks the chalgija players for a heartfelt song of longing; it is a feast day for his soul, yet today he weeps for his lost youth. Verse 2: he asks them to play everything from the heart; the same feast-day-and-grief contrast, this time grieving. Verse 3: he asks for a song as heavy as life itself, to fill his eyes with tears and ease his heart. Verse 4: the refrain returns and he mourns his youth. Grammar: the plural imperatives засвирете and свирете take the short pronoun ми (for me); the final да олеснат is a plural subjunctive, literally 'so that they ease'.
