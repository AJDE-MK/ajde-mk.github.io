# History, places and customs

Background for the songs. The Ilinden part comes from your songbook; the rest comes from Qwen's draft and has not been checked.

## The Ilinden songs

These songs come out of the struggle against Ottoman rule, especially the Ilinden Uprising of August 1903. Rebels met at the Smilevo Congress that spring to plan it, and the town of Kruševo declared a short-lived republic before Ottoman forces crushed it after about ten days. The fighters were called *komiti*. Ilinden (St. Elijah's Day, August 2) is still Macedonia's national holiday, and these songs work as oral history of that time.

Names that appear in the songs: Goce Delčev (killed in 1903), Nikola Karev, Pitu Guli, Damjan Gruev and Jane Sandanski. IMRO is the secret revolutionary organisation behind the uprising.

## Pechalba, the songs of leaving

*Pechalba* means going abroad to work and send money home. It is a big part of Macedonian life of the last 150 years: first to Turkey, then across the ocean to America and Australia after the wars, then to Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Whole families were split for years, and the songs were the phone calls. Waiting at home, the writing of letters, the steamship at the dock and the train to Germany all come back in the songs.

## The ritual year

| When | Custom | Songs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christmas Eve to Epiphany | *коледување*, carolling men | carols |
| 13 and 14 January (St. Basil) | *василица* | New Year songs |
| Lazarus Saturday | *лазарки*, unmarried girls | Lazarus songs |
| St. George's Day | shepherds' day | herding songs |
| Summer | harvest, and the evening water-fetching (*водарки*) | harvest and well songs |
| Autumn and winter | cycles of the *samodiva* spirits | ballads |

Song types to look for: ritual (tied to a date), wedding (tied to a moment of the three-day wedding), *oro* dance tunes named after a town, and children's songs and animal farces such as the rabbit's wedding.
