# How to read this songbook

Every song page has the Cyrillic text, a Latin version you can read by ear, English where available, and listening links.

## Where the English comes from

- **EN** in the index: Pesna.org's own English translation.
- **EN\***: my own machine translation, written for this book. It has not been checked by a native speaker. Lines I was guessing are marked "my reading" in the notes.
- A few songs with a named, recent author have a summary, a word list and grammar notes instead of full English.

## Transliteration key

Macedonian is spelled as it sounds, so the Latin column lets you follow along by ear.

| Cyrillic | Latin | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ч | č | ch in church |
| ш | š | sh in ship |
| ж | ž | s in measure |
| џ | dž | j in jam |
| ѓ | ǵ | soft gy, between g and j |
| ќ | ḱ | soft ky, between k and ch |
| ѕ | dz | ds in kids |
| ј | j | y in yes |
| љ / њ | lj / nj | lli in million / ny in canyon |
| х | h | ch in Scottish loch |

## Variations between versions

Traditional lyrics vary by village and singer. The versions here are the forms Pesna.org holds, and a recording may add, drop or reorder verses. Where Pesna.org holds two versions, the page shows both.

## Finding a recording

The YouTube links on the pages are searches, not single videos, because specific video links go dead. A search trick: paste the song's Cyrillic title plus "текст" into Google or YouTube to find Macedonian lyric pages and lyric videos.
