# Кажи облаче ле бело

*Kaži oblače le belo* · *Tell me, white cloud*

Pesna.org #843: <https://pesna.org/song/843>

**Notes.** Автор на текстот на песната е поетот Ганчо Негенцов (со псевдоним „Ран Босилек“), која ја напишал во 1916 година, додека студирал право во Брисел. Подоцна песната добила мелодија и се распространила низ Македонија и Бугарија.

## Background

The text was written by the poet Gancho Negentsov, who wrote under the pen name Ran Bosilek, in 1916 while he was studying law in Brussels. Later the song got a melody and spread through Macedonia and Bulgaria. Mood: homesick, tender, hopeful.

*Source of these notes: the note on Pesna.org.*

## English

*A composed song with a known author, so there is no line-by-line English here: a summary and a word list instead, so you can follow along.*

**Your translation.** Written by you, not by me. I checked it line by line against the Cyrillic.

> Verse 1 (the exile speaks):
> Tell me, tell me, white little cloud,
> where have you been, why are you so sad?
> Did you see my native village,
> did you hear what my mother says?
>
> Verse 2 (the cloud answers):
> I saw your native village,
> and I heard your mother speaking:
> "My dear child is in a foreign land,
> sharing foreign bread with strangers."
>
> Verse 3 (the exile replies):
> Go, tell my old mother
> that you have seen me still alive.
> A day will come when I return home,
> to return home and see my mother.
> A day will come when I return home,
> to return home and embrace my beloved.

**What it is about.** Author of the text: the Bulgarian poet Gancho Negentsov (pen name Ran Bosilek), written in 1916 while he studied law in Brussels; a melody was added later and the song spread through Macedonia and Bulgaria (per the input notes). Mood: homesick, tender, hopeful. Verse 1: the exile asks a white cloud where it has been and why it looks sad, and whether it saw his native village and heard what his mother says. Verse 2: the cloud answers that it saw the village and heard the mother say her dear child is abroad, sharing bread with strangers. Verse 3: the exile tells the cloud to carry word to his old mother that he is alive, and promises that a day will come when he returns home to see his mother and embrace his sweetheart. Grammar and dialect: the neuter forms бело, невесело, видело agree with облаче (a neuter diminutive), while делило agrees with чедо (child, also neuter); the apostrophes in дал', в' mark clipped forms; the question mother/mother-of-mine is expressed with the postposed clitic (мајка ми, мајка ти).
