# Macedonian Folk Songbook

Oct 17, 2026 · @Mike

## How to use this songbook

Traditional songs have full text in three columns: Cyrillic, transliteration, and English. Composed folk-style songs have background, a recording, and a link to a lyrics page, but no reproduced text.

Traditional lyrics vary by village and singer. The versions here are common standard forms; a recording may add, drop, or reorder verses. English translations are my own and lean literal so you can match line to line.

**Transliteration key.** Macedonian is spelled as it sounds, so the Latin column lets you follow along by ear.

| Cyrillic | Latin | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ч | č | ch in church |
| ш | š | sh in ship |
| ж | ž | s in measure |
| џ | dž | j in jam |
| ѓ | ǵ | soft gy, between g and j |
| ќ | ḱ | soft ky, between k and ch |
| ѕ | dz | ds in kids |
| ј | j | y in yes |
| љ / њ | lj / nj | lli in million / ny in canyon |
| х | h | ch in Scottish loch |

**Search trick.** Paste a song's Cyrillic title plus "текст" into Google or YouTube to find Macedonian lyric pages and lyric videos.

## Traditional love songs

### Јовано, Јованке (Jovano, Jovanke)

**Background.** A traditional song about two young lovers kept apart by the girl's disapproving parents ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovano_Jovanke)). She bleaches linen on the banks of the Vardar, the river that runs through the middle of Macedonia. It is sung across the Balkans, in North Macedonia, Bulgaria, Greek Macedonia, and former Yugoslavia, and is probably the most-covered Macedonian song. *Mori* and *dušo* are endearments, roughly "hey girl" and "my soul."

### Билјана платно белеше (Biljana Was Bleaching Linen)

**Background.** Set at the springs of Lake Ohrid. Biljana is bleaching linen for her dowry when a caravan of wine merchants passes. She falls for the young man leading it, only to learn he is getting married that Sunday ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biljana_platno_bele%C5%A1e)). Versions appear in folk-song collections from 1860 and 1861, and the motif of a girl and passing merchants runs from Ohrid to the Black Sea. Belograd is the old Slavic name for Berat in Albania. The last verse is a playful curse: may the river carry him to her, may a snake bite him with her teeth.

## Traditional laments and historical songs

### Зајди, зајди јасно сонце (Set, Set, Bright Sun)

**Background.** A lament for lost youth, not for a lover. The singer tells the sun and moon to go dark and calls the forest her sister. The forest's leaves will grow back each spring; her youth never will. It is one of the most performed slow songs in the repertoire, recorded by everyone from Aleksandar Sarievski to Toše Proeski.

**Listen:** [YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Zajdi+zajdi+jasno+sonce+Sarievski) · [Pesna.org page with 17 audio samples](https://pesna.org/song/452)

### Ајде слушај, слушај, калеш бре Анѓо (Listen, Dark-Eyed Anǵa)

**Background.** A defiance song from Ottoman times, when Macedonia was under Turkish rule (roughly the late 1300s to 1912). A Turk courts Anǵa with promises: she'll sit in a fine *čardak* (an enclosed upper balcony), count gold coins, and string pearls for her neck. In the last verse she answers him: she won't be a Turkish wife, and she doesn't understand Turkish. Refusing marriage meant refusing conversion, so the song is remembered as a statement of faith and identity. *Kaleš* is a Turkish loanword, usually rendered "dark-eyed" or "black-haired." *Anama* means a Turkish lady or wife.

## Children's and comic songs

### Сторил ниет зајко, зајко кокорајко (The Rabbit Decided to Marry)

**Background.** Your memory is right: it's the rabbit's wedding day. A dandy rabbit dresses up and gets engaged to a widowed fox. He invites an animal wedding party: a she-bear, a she-wolf as godmother, a frog on the *zurla* (folk oboe), a hedgehog on the drum. He crosses the plain of Solun (Thessaloniki) to fetch his bride, finds a mother hen with chicks instead, runs into hunters, and bolts, shouting that he's no bridegroom. It's a parody of a village wedding, and kids love it. *Kokorajko* is a playful rhyming nickname with no exact meaning, roughly "cocky little bunny." The song is packed with Turkish loanwords ending in *-džija* (one who does a job), a trace of Ottoman-era speech.

Pesna.org lists two versions. This is the fuller one, which matches most recordings.

## Revolutionary and Ilinden songs

These songs come out of the struggle against Ottoman rule, especially the Ilinden Uprising of August 1903. Rebels met at the Smilevo Congress that spring to plan it, and the town of Kruševo declared a short-lived republic before Ottoman forces crushed it after about ten days. The fighters were called *komiti*. Ilinden (St. Elijah's Day, August 2) is still Macedonia's national holiday, and these songs work as oral history of that time.

### Крушево абер пристигна (Word Came to Kruševo)

**Background.** A call goes out for families to send their grown sons to fight at Smilevo. Stojan has no sons, only his daughter Todorka. She asks her father for a Berdan rifle (*berdanka*, a Russian-made rifle common among the rebels) and a saber, joins the *komiti*, and fights for three years without anyone realizing she's a girl. Pesna.org has no English translation of this one.

## Composed folk-style songs Dad probably played

These songs have known authors and are still under copyright, so their lyrics aren't reproduced here. Each Pesna.org link below opens the full Cyrillic text, a transliteration, and usually an English translation, plus audio samples to read along with.

**Jonče Hristovski (1931–2000)** is the central figure here. Born in Bitola, he was a singer and composer of folk music who also performed with the Tanec national ensemble ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon%C4%8De_Hristovski)). His wife died in the 1963 Skopje earthquake after giving birth to their twin daughters. A year later he wrote his best-known song.

I've only listed songs whose authorship I confirmed. Many other Pesna.org favorites, like *Bitola, moj roden kraj*, *Biser balkanski*, and *Zemjo Makedonska*, may also be composed songs, but I haven't verified their authors yet.
